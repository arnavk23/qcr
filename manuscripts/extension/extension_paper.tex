\title{\textbf{Local Circuit Rewriting, Made Exact}\\[4pt]
\large Equivalence as a group action, certified ground truth, and a hardware-aware optimiser}
\author{Draft extension of Rosenhahn, Osborne \& Hirche, arXiv:2502.14715}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Local term-replacement methods shorten a quantum circuit by repeatedly replacing a short sub-circuit with a cheaper
equivalent one from a precomputed database. Rosenhahn, Osborne and Hirche~\cite{R25} recently proposed such a method and
compared it with Qiskit. We make three contributions.
(i) \emph{A framework.} Every useful notion of ``equivalent sub-circuit'' (exact, up to global phase, on the $|0\rangle$ input,
before a computational-basis measurement, up to a symmetry of the chip, within $\varepsilon$) is the orbit relation of a group
action on unitaries. The database is then keyed by a canonical form of the orbit, and soundness and error accumulation follow
from two short propositions.
(ii) \emph{A general optimiser.} It makes no assumption about the gate set. It uses hashed databases of up to $3.2\times10^7$
three-qubit classes, meet-in-the-middle lookup, exact two-qubit resynthesis, and DAG-convex windows restricted to coupled
qubits. It optimises a calibrated cost ($-\log$ fidelity per gate), which we select over gate count and two-qubit-weighted
count by noisy simulation.
(iii) \emph{Exact ground truth.} Every gate in~\cite{R25} is Clifford, so its benchmarks live in a finite group that we enumerate
completely (92,897,280 three-qubit elements). This gives proven optima, an exactness theorem for local rewriting, a parity
theorem, and a proof that random-circuit benchmarks converge to a known constant.
Against this ground truth, the general optimiser (which does not use it) reaches the proven optimum on
\TThreeAtOpt/20 and \TFourAtOpt/20 three-qubit benchmark circuits and produces \TThreeGenOverPaper$\times$ and \TFourGenOverPaper$\times$
the length of the original method's output. On \HwCompared\ algorithm circuits (Nam/VOQC, QASMBench, QFT/QAOA/Trotter) compiled for an IBM Heron
model, it removes 13\% of the gates of Qiskit level-3 output without ever adding a two-qubit gate. Its output has a lower noisy-simulation error than
Qiskit level 3 on \HeronCalBeatsLThree/\HeronTvdCircuits\ circuits. All \PaperBenchN\ paper-benchmark outputs and all 720 hardware-suite outputs are verified numerically.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Compiling a circuit into a device's native gates leaves redundancy, and on noisy hardware every gate costs fidelity.
Rosenhahn, Osborne and Hirche~\cite{R25} proposed a simple, gate-set-agnostic remedy: view the circuit as a word over the
native alphabet, sample a window of $2$--$8$ letters, and replace it by a shorter equivalent word, found by random sampling (V1),
by lookup in an exhaustively enumerated database of short circuits (V2), or by a lookup gated by a random forest (V3). The method
belongs to a long line of database-driven and peephole optimisers~\cite{prasad2006,golubitsky2012,nam2018,iten2022,quartz,queso,quarl,bravyi2021templates}.

Two questions decide whether such a method is worth using, and~\cite{R25} answers neither. \emph{How far from optimal is it?} The paper
compares only with Qiskit, so it cannot say whether its 45-gate output for a 3-qubit circuit is good (the optimum is about 10).
\emph{Does it help on circuits and hardware that matter?} The paper's alphabets are discrete $\pm\pi/2$ rotations on all-to-all
connectivity. Its benchmarks are random words over those alphabets, and its objective is the total gate count.

This paper answers both questions. The answers rest on two pieces that we keep strictly separate:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item a \textbf{general optimiser} (\S\ref{sec:framework}--\ref{sec:engine}) that uses no structure of the gate set. It runs on continuous-angle native gates,
respects connectivity, and supports exact, state-aware and approximate equivalence;
\item an \textbf{exact ground truth} (\S\ref{sec:theory}--\ref{sec:exact}) that \emph{does} use structure. All gates of~\cite{R25} are Clifford, so their circuits
are elements of a finite group whose Cayley graph we enumerate completely. We use it only to evaluate; the optimiser never sees it.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item A unifying model: equivalence notions are group actions, and database keys are canonical orbit representatives
  (Table~\ref{tab:actions}). Soundness of state- and measurement-aware rewriting and an additive error bound for approximate rewriting
  follow from this model (Props.~\ref{prop:sound},~\ref{prop:approx}).
\item A general, connectivity-aware optimiser with exact global-phase handling and a calibrated cost model (\S\ref{sec:engine}).
\item Theory for the discrete case: an exactness threshold (local = global optimality), a parity theorem, mixing of random-circuit
  benchmarks, and universal counting lower bounds (\S\ref{sec:theory}).
\item Exact ground truth for every 3-qubit circuit over both alphabets of~\cite{R25}, and certified bounds for 4 qubits (\S\ref{sec:exact}).
\item Experiments: optimality gaps on the paper's own benchmarks, a hardware suite on IBM Heron and trapped-ion models against Qiskit and TKET,
  selection of the objective by noisy simulation, and every output verified (\S\ref{sec:exp}).
\item An explicit list of the original paper's weaknesses and of the limitations of ours (\S\ref{sec:limits}, \S\ref{sec:weak}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Framework}\label{sec:framework}
\subsection{Circuits, devices, costs}
A \emph{device} fixes $n$ physical qubits, a coupling graph $E$ (or all-to-all), native single-qubit gates, a native two-qubit gate allowed only on
pairs in $E$, and which angles are continuous. We use two models.
\textbf{IBM Heron} (Qiskit's \texttt{FakeTorino}, 133 qubits, heavy-hex): $\RZ(\theta)$ (virtual), $\SX$, $X$, $\CZ$, with per-qubit and per-pair
calibrated errors. Eleven couplers have calibrated error $1.0$ and are removed.
\textbf{Trapped ion} (all-to-all): $\RX,\RY,\RZ(\theta)$ and the M{\o}lmer--S{\o}rensen gate $\RXX(\theta)$ at any angle. No public
calibration exists, so we use model errors $3\cdot10^{-5}$ (1q), $10^{-3}$ (2q), and treat $\RZ$ as virtual.
A circuit is a word $u=\gamma_1\cdots\gamma_L$ of native gates with $\ev(u)=\gamma_L\cdots\gamma_1$. A \emph{cost} is additive,
$c(u)=\sum_i c(\gamma_i)$. We compare
\[
c_{\rm count}\equiv1,\qquad c_{w2}=\begin{cases}10&\text{2q}\\1&\text{1q}\end{cases},\qquad c_{\rm cal}(\gamma)=-\log\bigl(1-e(\gamma)\bigr),
\]
where $e(\gamma)$ is the calibrated error of $\gamma$ on its physical qubits. Then $\exp(-c_{\rm cal}(u))$ is the estimated success probability
(ESP)~\cite{nishio2020}: minimising $c_{\rm cal}$ maximises ESP. Virtual gates get a tie-breaking cost of $10^{-7}$.

\subsection{Equivalence as a group action}
A replacement $u\to v$ of a window is \emph{sound} if it does not change what the full program computes. What counts as ``the same''
depends on context, and each notion we need is the orbit relation of a group $H$ acting on $U(2^k)$:
\begin{table}[H]\centering\small
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\begin{tabular}{llll}\toprule
notion & $U\sim V$ iff & group acting & canonical key\\\midrule
exact & $V=U$ & trivial & $U$\\
up to phase (as in~\cite{R25}) & $V=e^{i\varphi}U$ & $U(1)$, left & phase-normalised $U$\\
input-aware (at circuit start) & $V|0\rangle=e^{i\varphi}U|0\rangle$ & stabiliser of $|0\rangle$; $U(1)$ & phase-normalised $U|0\rangle$\\
output-aware (before measurement) & $V=DU$, $D$ diagonal & diagonal unitaries, left & rows phase-normalised\\
symmetry & $V=P_\pi UP_\pi^{\dagger}$ & coupling-graph automorphisms & minimum over the orbit\\
approximate & $\op{U-V}\le\varepsilon$ & none (a tolerance) & exact key, verified distance\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\caption{Equivalence notions. The database is keyed by a hash of the canonical form, so one lookup finds every member of the orbit.
Notions compose: for example, output-aware equivalence modulo automorphisms uses keys minimised over the automorphism group of row-normalised matrices.}
\label{tab:actions}
\end{table}
Exact equivalence including global phase is the only notion that stays sound when the circuit is later controlled or used as a
sub-routine. ``Up to phase'' is what~\cite{R25} uses. We keep it only to compare with that paper.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of state- and measurement-aware rewriting]\label{prop:sound}
Let the program be ``prepare $|0^n\rangle$, apply $\ev(u)$, measure all qubits in the computational basis''. Let $S$ be a convex window of
$u$ on wires $W$.
(a) If no gate precedes $S$ on any wire in $W$ and $\ev(v)|0_W\rangle=e^{i\varphi}\ev(S)|0_W\rangle$, replacing $S$ by $v$ does not change
the output distribution.
(b) If no gate follows $S$ on any wire in $W$ and $\ev(v)=D\,\ev(S)$ with $D$ diagonal, replacing $S$ by $v$ does not change the output distribution.
Any sequence of such rewrites, each applied to the current circuit, preserves the output distribution.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By convexity we may move $S$ to the front (a) or the back (b) of the circuit without changing $\ev(u)$.
(a) The input factorises as $|0_W\rangle\otimes|0_{\bar W}\rangle$, so the final state changes only by $e^{i\varphi}$.
(b) The final state changes from $|\psi\rangle$ to $(D\otimes I)|\psi\rangle$, and $|\langle x|(D\otimes I)|\psi\rangle|^2=|\langle x|\psi\rangle|^2$
for every basis state $x$. The invariant is the output distribution of the current circuit, so soundness composes.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Approximate rewriting]\label{prop:approx}
If rewrites $i=1,\dots,m$ replace windows $S_i$ by $v_i$ with $\op{\ev(v_i)-\ev(S_i)}\le\varepsilon_i$ in operator norm, then the final
circuit $u'$ satisfies $\op{\ev(u')-\ev(u)}\le\sum_i\varepsilon_i$. In exact mode this includes global phase.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Telescope over intermediate circuits. Each step changes one factor of a product of unitaries, and
$\op{A(X-Y)B}=\op{X-Y}$ for unitary $A,B$.
\end{proof}
The optimiser tracks $\sum_i\varepsilon_i$ and reports it with the circuit. Equivalence tests are \emph{linear} in the operator error
(norm distance, not $|\mathrm{tr}(U^\dagger V)|/2^k\approx1$, which accepts errors of order $\sqrt{\mathrm{tol}}$).

\subsection{Local rewriting}
\begin{definition}[Local rewriting]
With window bound $w$ and database radius $r$,
$\mathcal R(w,r)=\{u\to v:|u|\le w,\ \ev(u)\sim\ev(v),\ |v|\le r,\ c(v)<c(u)\}$. Two gates are \emph{independent} if they commute exactly.
Words equal up to swapping adjacent independent gates are \emph{trace-equivalent}~\cite{cartier,diekert}. A word is $(w,r)$-\emph{reduced}
if no trace-equivalent word has a factor that is the left side of a rule.
\end{definition}
The factors of trace-equivalent words are exactly the \emph{convex} subsets of the circuit DAG (a set is convex iff it is an interval of some
topological order). V2 of~\cite{R25} applies $\mathcal R(8,5)$ to random contiguous factors and explores the trace class by random commuting
swaps, which amounts to a stochastic enumeration of convex windows. We enumerate convex windows directly instead.

\section{The optimiser}\label{sec:engine}
\paragraph{Databases.} For each device we fix a discrete grid of native gates on a 3-qubit local topology (all pairs for ions, the line $0$--$1$--$2$
for heavy-hex). Heron: $\RZ\in\{\pm\frac\pi4,\pm\frac\pi2,\pi\}$, $\SX$, $X$, $\CZ$ (23 gates). Ion: $\RX,\RY,\RZ$ on the same angles and
$\RXX(\pm\frac\pi4,\pm\frac\pi2)$ (57 gates). We enumerate words breadth-first and store, for each new class, a 64-bit hash of its canonical
form (Table~\ref{tab:actions}), minimised over the topology's automorphisms. The deepest level stores keys only. Every hit is re-checked
numerically before use.

\paragraph{Lookup beyond the radius.} If a window $U$ is not in the database, meet-in-the-middle tries $U=R\,A$ with $A$ in a stored full ball of
radius~2. This extends the reach by two gates at the cost of one hash lookup per element of that ball.
\paragraph{Continuous angles.} Circuits from real compilers use arbitrary angles, which no finite grid contains. Windows on at most two
\emph{coupled} wires are therefore also resynthesised analytically. On Heron we use KAK into $\RZ/\SX/X/\CZ$ with the minimal number of $\CZ$.
On ions, a Weyl decomposition yields one $\RXX(\theta)$ per non-zero interaction coefficient. Single-qubit runs use Euler decompositions.
\paragraph{Windows.} From each position $i$, a window grows by collecting later gates on a set of $\le3$ wires that commute (exactly, tolerance
$10^{-10}$) past the gates they jump over. The result is a convex set. On Heron the wires must induce a connected subgraph, so every replacement
is legal without routing. Every prefix of the window is tried, and the replacement with the largest cost saving is applied. Sweeps alternate with
random exact-commutation shuffles until $p$ consecutive shuffles bring no gain ($p=4$ on the hardware suite; $p=8$ with 4 restarts on
the paper's benchmarks).
\paragraph{Global phase.} Inside windows the optimiser matches up to phase. It records each replacement's phase, snapped to the set the device can
realise exactly ($\pi/4$ multiples on Heron, $\pi/2$ on ions). It then repairs the accumulated phase at the end with the cheapest gadget, or by absorbing
$\pm i$ into an existing $\RXX$ on ions. Output is therefore equal \emph{including} global phase. Qiskit and TKET store the phase as metadata
instead, so we also report that convention.
\paragraph{Guarantee.} The optimiser never returns a circuit of higher cost than its input: if repair would exceed the savings, it returns the input.

\section{Theory for discrete gate sets}\label{sec:theory}
The following results concern the alphabets of~\cite{R25}:
$\Gamma_{\rm ion}=\{\RX,\RY,\RZ(\pm\frac\pi2)\}\cup\{\RXX(\pm\frac\pi2)\}$ and $\Gamma_{\rm ibm}=\{\RX,\RZ(\pm\frac\pi2)\}\cup\{\CZ\}$, modulo phase.
\begin{lemma}[The alphabets generate the Clifford group]
$\langle\Gamma\rangle=\Cl_n/U(1)$, of order $2^{n^2+2n}\prod_{j=1}^n(4^j-1)$. Verified by enumeration for $n\le3$ (24; 11,520; 92,897,280).
The compute graph of depth $d$ in~\cite{R25} is the ball $B(d)$ of $\Cay(G,\Gamma)$. All 22 entries of~\cite[Table I]{R25} are reproduced exactly.
\end{lemma}
Write $|g|$ for the word length, $S(r)$ for the sphere, and $D$ for the diameter.

\subsection{Exactness threshold and cascade}
\begin{theorem}[Local = global above the diameter]\label{thm:exact}
Let $G$ be finite with diameter $D$. If $w\ge D+1$ and $r\ge D$, every $(w,r)$-reduced word is a geodesic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $u$ be $(w,r)$-reduced. If $|u|>D$, its first $D+1$ letters form a factor $f$ with $|\ev(f)|\le D\le r$ and $|f|\le w$, so a rule applies, a
contradiction. Hence $|u|\le D<w$, so $u$ is itself a factor of length $\le w$, and if it were not geodesic the rule $u\to v$ with $v$ geodesic would apply.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Cascade]\label{prop:cascade}
Let $\ell(w)$ be the length of the fixed point of an exhaustive $\mathcal R(w,\infty)$ optimiser. If $\ell(w)\le w$ at any time, the next sweep returns a geodesic.
\end{proposition}
So the relevant window is $w^\star=\min\{w:\ell(w)\le w\}$, which we measure: $w^\star\approx\WStarIon$ (ion trap) and $\WStarIbm$ (IBM-style) for 3-qubit
circuits, well below $D+1=\DiamIonThreePlusOne$. For continuous gate sets $G$ is infinite, $D=\infty$, and no finite window is exact. This is why
ground truth must come from the discrete case.

\subsection{Gate-count parity}
\begin{theorem}[Parity]\label{thm:parity}
(a) For $n\in\{1,2\}$ and both alphabets, $\Cay(\Cl_n,\Gamma)$ is bipartite, so every equivalent rewrite removes an even number of gates.
(b) For $n\ge3$ and any generating set of $\Cl_n/U(1)$ that contains, for each qubit, a gate squaring to $X_i$ and one squaring to $Z_i$, the
Cayley graph is not bipartite.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A Cayley graph is bipartite iff some homomorphism $\chi:G\to\{\pm1\}$ has $\chi(\Gamma)=-1$. (a) For $n=1$, $\Cl_1/U(1)\cong S_4$, every
$\pm\frac\pi2$ rotation is a 4-cycle, and $\chi=\mathrm{sgn}$. For $n=2$ we verify it exhaustively. (b) The Pauli group is normal with quotient
$\Sp(2n,2)$. Since $\chi(X_i)=\chi(\gamma)^2=1$ (and likewise $Z_i$), $\chi$ factors through $\Sp(2n,2)$, which is simple and non-abelian for $n\ge3$. Hence $\chi\equiv1$.
\end{proof}
The shortest odd relation for $n=3$ has length $\rho=\OddRelIon$ (ion trap) and $\OddRelIbm$ (IBM-style). A parity change therefore needs a window of
at least $\lceil\rho/2\rceil=\OddRelIonHalf$ gates, and Fig.~4 of~\cite{R25}, a $6\to3$ replacement on two qubits, cannot come from the stated gate set.

\subsection{What random-circuit benchmarks measure}
\begin{theorem}[Benchmarks mix]\label{thm:mix}
Let $\Gamma=\Gamma^{-1}$, $\mu_L$ the law of $\ev(u)$ for $u$ uniform in $\Gamma^L$, and $\beta<1$ the largest non-trivial eigenvalue modulus of
the random walk (excluding $-1$ if bipartite). Then $\|\mu_L-\pi_L\|_{TV}\le\frac12\sqrt{|G|}\,\beta^L$, with $\pi_L$ uniform on $G$ (or on the parity
class). Hence $\mathbb E|\ev(u)|\to\frac1{|G|}\sum_d d\,|S(d)|$.
\end{theorem}
This is the standard $L^2$ bound~\cite{diaconis}. For $n=2$ (ion trap) $\beta=\BetaTwo$. Consequently a random length-300 benchmark has a
\emph{constant} optimal answer: \MeanIonThree\ gates (3 qubits, ion trap) and \MeanIbmThree\ (IBM-style), independent of the input length beyond $L\approx\MixLThree$.

\subsection{Lower bounds for every method}
\begin{proposition}[Counting bound]\label{prop:count}
For $g$ uniform in $G$, $\Pr[|g|\le d]\le|B(d)|/|G|$, and $|S(a+b)|\le|S(a)||S(b)|$. Exact spheres up to depth $k$ thus give lower bounds on
$\mathbb E|g|$ for every method.
\end{proposition}
At $n=4$ ($|G|=1.21\times10^{13}$), spheres to depth 6 give $\mathbb E|g|\ge\LBFourIon$ (ion trap) and $\ge\LBFourIbm$ (IBM-style).
At $n=3$ the same bound gives $\LBThreeCheck$, against a true value of \MeanIonThree.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
\paragraph{Verification.} Every output is checked against its input. Exact mode: $\op{(U-V)\psi}$ on random states (statevector on the active
qubits). State-aware mode: the total-variation distance (TVD) of the output distributions on $|0^n\rangle$. Approximate mode: the measured
error is compared with the tracked bound. Every output is also checked to be native and to use only coupled pairs.

Python and single-threaded. Qiskit L3 takes well under a second.

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Verification.} Verification is numerical (random input states), not symbolic. On Heron, the routed layout of Nam pf2\_10 spans 20
  physical qubits, more than the main run's statevector cap of 16, so its five L1-based outputs were verified in a separate re-run (same totals;
  exact error $\le3.3\cdot10^{-11}$, approximate error $1.3\cdot10^{-4}$ within its bound $7.1\cdot10^{-4}$). All other \HeronRuns\ (Heron) and \IonRuns\ (ion) outputs, and the \PaperBenchN\ paper-benchmark
  outputs, were verified in the run itself (exact error $\le\IonMaxExactErr$, state-aware TVD $\le\IonMaxStateErr$).
\item \textbf{Noise models, not hardware.} ESP and TVD come from calibration snapshots and simulators. The Heron model contains gate errors only:
  no readout, idle (decoherence during waiting), or crosstalk errors. The ion parameters are assumptions.
\item \textbf{Global phase.} Our outputs are exact including phase. On Heron this costs \HeronPhaseGadgetGates\ single-qubit gates over the suite. With phase as
  metadata, as Qiskit and TKET store it, our all-gate ratio from L1 improves from $\times0.838$ to $\times0.825$.
\item \textbf{Baselines.} BQSKit was not available in our environment, and Quartz/QUESO were not run. Qiskit and TKET are used at their
  defaults with one seed. Layout and routing are Qiskit's, and our optimiser does not change the layout.
\item \textbf{Scale.} Windows have at most 3 qubits, and the code is single-threaded Python. Circuits beyond $\sim$1,000 gates were not tested.
\item \textbf{Feed-forward.} Circuits with mid-circuit measurement and classical control are out of scope.
\end{itemize}

\section{Weaknesses of the original paper}\label{sec:weak}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Missing structure.} The gate sets are Clifford, so the search space is a finite group with exact fingerprints and a complete
  theory~\cite{aaronson2004,selinger2015,bravyi2022six}. None of it is used. The paper uses floating-point comparisons with inconsistent tolerances
  ($10^{-5}$ in the text, $10^{-3}$/$10^{-4}$ in the code) and an $O(N)$ linear scan where an $O(1)$ key exists. It then adds a classifier (V3) to save time on that lookup.
\item \textbf{No notion of optimality.} The paper compares only with Qiskit. Its 3-qubit result is \GapFactorIon$\times$ the optimum, which can be computed in minutes.
\item \textbf{Equivalence notion.} Up-to-phase replacement is unsound under control, and the paper does not discuss it. Neither the state/measurement context
  nor approximation is used, and no error is tracked.
\item \textbf{Hardware model.} Discrete $\pm\pi/2$ angles, all-to-all connectivity, and total gate count as the objective. None of these matches IBM
  ($\RZ(\theta)$, heavy-hex) or ion (continuous MS) hardware.
\item \textbf{Evaluation protocol.} Random circuits mix (Thm.~\ref{thm:mix}), so ``from 300 gates'' measures the input length. Tables III--V use one
  circuit each. Times compare MATLAB with compiled transpilers.
\item \textbf{Search.} Windows are contiguous. Commutation is explored by random swaps, with no convergence criterion. The radius-5 database misses
  windows whose optimum is 6--7 gates.
\item \textbf{V3.} Features, forest and threshold are unreported. Reproduced recall is 30\% at the default threshold.
\item \textbf{Hardware evidence.} One 2-qubit circuit, one run per chip, no error bars. In our reproduction the effect disappears at Qiskit level 3.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility.} The released code contains only V2 and has visible bugs (\texttt{simplereduce} skips the last pair; the database excludes the
  identity). The database used for Tables III--V is not stated.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:agenda}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Local-to-global theory.} Characterise $w^\star(G,\Gamma)$. Prove the cascade threshold for random inputs from the growth series, and relate
  it to Dehn presentations and $k$-local geodesics~\cite{gromov,epstein}.
\item \textbf{Ground truth beyond Clifford.} With $\pm\pi/4$ angles the group is infinite (Clifford+T). Exact normal forms~\cite{giles2013,kmm2013}
  could still give certified optima for small $T$-counts.
\item \textbf{Larger windows.} 4-qubit databases via symmetry reduction, or a learned admissible heuristic for A$^\ast$ trained on exact tables.
\item \textbf{Layout-aware rewriting.} Joint optimisation of layout and local rewriting, using calibrated costs.
\item \textbf{Real hardware.} Runs with error bars at fixed transpiler settings, reporting two-qubit counts and ESP.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
Local rewriting becomes reliable when ``equivalent'' is made precise. Each notion is a group action, the database stores its orbits, and
soundness and error accumulation follow. A general optimiser built this way, without using the structure of any gate set, reaches proven optima on
the original paper's 3-qubit benchmarks, at a quarter of the original method's output length. It also improves Qiskit level-3 output on real
algorithms for Heron and ion models without ever adding a two-qubit gate. The Clifford ground truth is what makes such claims checkable.
